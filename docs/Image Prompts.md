## Fire pit layout — full installation vision

Isometric axonometric site-plan illustration of the finished installation: a central fire pit with six painted columns arranged radially on drop-cloth "spokes," people painting and socializing, one garage door open with music playing.

> Isometric axonometric architectural drawing, ink line-art with soft watercolor washes in the style of a landscape architecture presentation rendering. A circular freestanding fire pit sits at the center of an open outdoor lot, surrounded by a small scatter of mismatched outdoor chairs — a couple of folding chairs, a wooden porch chair — with several people sitting and standing around it, some in conversation, some just relaxing by the fire. Render all the people with natural, believable proportions and relaxed, candid poses and gestures — not stiff or stick-figure-like — as if drawn from life, with individual character in their posture and clothing. Six canvas drop cloths, each 6 feet wide and 9 feet long, are laid flat on the ground radiating outward from the fire pit in six directions, spaced 60° apart like spokes of a wheel. Each drop cloth's inner short edge touches and abuts the outer rim of the fire pit, with no gap between cloth and fire pit; the cloth then runs the full 9 feet outward to its far short edge. One tall cylindrical column, 8 feet tall and 12 inches in diameter, stands directly on top of the far end of each drop cloth — the column's base sits centered on the cloth's outer edge, so the cloth catches any paint that drips down the column while someone paints it. Each column is hand-painted in folk-art patterns — blue waves, orange suns, leafy shapes — with a paint bucket holding brushes set on the drop cloth beside its base. Two different people, each at a different column, stand painting with brushes in hand, in natural mid-motion poses, each standing on their column's drop cloth. The composition reads as a radial site plan: fire pit as the hub, six columns as the rim, and six drop cloths as unbroken spokes physically connecting the fire pit to each column. Along one edge of the lot runs a single wall of garage doors, all closed except one, which stands open with music playing out from inside. Warehouse district setting, gravel and pavement ground, soft late-afternoon light, warm muted palette, clean architectural linework, true isometric angles with no perspective distortion so all six spokes and columns read at consistent scale.
