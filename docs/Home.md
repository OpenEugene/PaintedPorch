# Home

The Painted Porch is a community space built on the idea that art and ideas should be free to anyone who walks up — not just the invited.

## Where the name comes from

Around 460 BC, the Athenians built the *Stoa Poikile* — the "Painted Porch" — a public colonnade on the north side of the Ancient Agora, its walls covered in paintings by Polygnotos and Mikon depicting the Battle of Marathon, the Battle of Oenoe, the fall of Troy, and the Battle of Thermopylae. Around 300 BC, Zeno of Citium started teaching philosophy under that porch. Anyone passing through the Agora could stop and listen — no membership, no invitation, unlike the more formal, gated schools and salons of the day. Zeno's followers became known as the *Stoics*, simply for meeting at the Painted Porch.

This space takes its name from that same idea: a porch, open to the street, where ideas and art move freely instead of staying locked inside a private circle.

## Part of the Warehouse District Art Hop

The Painted Porch is one stop on the [Warehouse District Art Hop](https://www.warehousedistrictarthop.com/), Eugene's monthly celebration of the west-side warehouse district's artists and creative spaces — alongside venues like Oblivion Gallery, Viking Brewing, and Pinebox.
